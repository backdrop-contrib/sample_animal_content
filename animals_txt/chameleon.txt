Chameleons are a distinctive group of lizards belonging to the family Chamaeleonidae, with over 200 known species found primarily in Africa and Madagascar. Famous for their ability to change color, chameleons are among the most visually remarkable reptiles on Earth.

Color change in chameleons is far more sophisticated than simple camouflage. Their skin contains layers of specialized cells called chromatophores, each containing different pigments, along with a layer of nanocrystals that reflect light. By adjusting the spacing of these crystals, chameleons can shift through a stunning range of colors. They use this ability primarily for communication — signaling mood, temperature, and reproductive status to other chameleons — rather than for hiding.

Chameleon eyes are perhaps their most extraordinary feature. Each eye moves independently, giving them a full 360-degree field of vision without moving their heads. When prey is spotted, both eyes lock onto the target to provide binocular depth perception, and the chameleon launches its tongue — a ballistic projectile that can extend to twice the body length in a fraction of a second, reaching accelerations of over 40 g.

Most chameleons are arboreal, their zygodactyl feet and prehensile tails perfectly adapted for gripping branches. They move with a characteristic slow, swaying gait that mimics leaves blowing in the wind. This deliberate movement, combined with their laterally compressed bodies and leaf-like profiles, makes them surprisingly difficult to spot in their forest habitats.
