Dolphins are among the most intelligent and charismatic animals in the ocean. Belonging to the family Delphinidae, these toothed whales are found in every ocean and in some of the world's major river systems.

The bottlenose dolphin is perhaps the best known species, recognized by its perpetual smile and playful nature. But the dolphin family is remarkably diverse, encompassing over 40 species ranging from the tiny Maui's dolphin at 1.2 meters to the orca, or killer whale, which can exceed 9 meters in length.

Dolphins navigate and hunt using echolocation, emitting rapid clicks that bounce off objects and return as echoes. This biological sonar is so precise that dolphins can detect the size, shape, speed, and even internal structure of objects in murky water. They also communicate with a rich repertoire of whistles and clicks, and each dolphin develops a unique signature whistle that functions like a name.

Social bonds are central to dolphin life. They live in groups called pods that cooperate in hunting, childcare, and defense. Dolphins have been observed teaching their young, using tools such as sponges to protect their snouts while foraging on the sea floor, and even playing games with other species. Their capacity for empathy, self-awareness, and cultural learning places them among the most cognitively sophisticated animals on the planet.
