Sea turtles are among the most ancient creatures on Earth, with a lineage stretching back over 100 million years. These remarkable reptiles spend nearly their entire lives in the ocean, navigating thousands of miles across open water using the Earth's magnetic field.

Seven species of sea turtle exist today, ranging from the small Kemp's ridley to the enormous leatherback, which can reach over 2 meters in length and weigh more than 900 kilograms. Unlike their land-dwelling relatives, sea turtles cannot retract into their shells, relying instead on their powerful flippers and streamlined bodies to escape predators.

Female sea turtles return to the same beaches where they were born to lay their eggs, a journey that may span entire ocean basins. After hatching, tiny turtles make a perilous dash to the sea, guided by the light of the moon reflecting off the water. Only about one in a thousand hatchlings survives to adulthood.

Sea turtles play a vital role in marine ecosystems. Green sea turtles graze on seagrass beds, keeping them healthy and productive. Hawksbills feed on sponges, preventing them from outcompeting corals on tropical reefs. Leatherbacks consume vast quantities of jellyfish, helping to regulate their populations.
